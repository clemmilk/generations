Rulebook

Generations

Each player rules a domain, represented by their deck of cards. Lineage after lineage, they transform it: they buy new cards, get rid of useless ones, and grow their population, their economy and their nobility. The first player to get through their whole deck in a single lineage wins.

2–4 players 30 minutes

1Goal of the game

The first player to draw the last card of their deck immediately wins the game.

At the start of the game, your deck has 11 cards. You will play through it several times, over several lineages. At the start of each new lineage, you shuffle all your cards and start again, with a slightly stronger domain.

2Components

  • 36starting cards: 9 per colour (blue, yellow, red, green). The 4 decks are identical.
  • 8Domain cards, dealt at random to make each deck different.
  • 34Advanced cards, which form the common deck.
  • 20Privilege tiles, numbered 1 to 20.
  • 4player boards, all identical.
  • 8markers: 2 per player, for the Population and Economy tracks.
  • —Gold coins, for each player's supply.

3Reading a card

  1. Resources. What the card provides once it is in your play area: Stone , Metal or Scholar . The Bank provides 1 Stone.
  2. Purchase cost. Only Advanced cards have one: Gold, or a Resource (Stone or Metal, your choice). The Bank costs 1 Resource.
  3. Banner. It shows where the card comes from: the player's colour for a starting card, purple for a Domain, black for an Advanced card. It has no effect on play.
  4. The card's name.
  5. Effect. What you may do when you play the card. Here: draw 1 card for every 2 Stone in your play area.
  6. Red Effect. Below the dotted line: what your opponents may do when you play the card. Here: pay 4 Gold to draw 1 card.

4Setup

  1. Each player takes the 9 starting cards of their colour. They add 2 Domain cards drawn at random, which they look at without showing them. They shuffle these 11 cards face down: this is their deck. Put the remaining Domains back in the box without looking at them (with 4 players, all 8 are dealt).

  2. Each player takes a player board and places their 2 markers on the starting space of the Population and Economy tracks.

  3. Shuffle the 34 Advanced cards face down: this is the common deck. Reveal the top 4 cards next to it: this is the River.

  4. Shuffle the 20 Privilege tiles face down, then reveal 4 of them face up.

  5. Choose a first player at random. As compensation, the 2nd player receives 1 Gold, the 3rd 2 Gold and the 4th 3 Gold.

  6. All players start their first lineage at the same time: each takes their Gold and draws their cards (see “Starting a lineage”). This Gold is added to their compensation.

5The player board

Your board has three tracks. They move up when you end a lineage, by one level at most per track, and make each of your following lineages stronger.

Left: Nobility

4 spaces for your Privilege tiles, from bottom to top. The number shows the Scholars required; the crowns show the Nobility crests gained.

Top right: Economy

On the left of each space, the Resources you need in your play area to move up; on the right, the Gold you receive at the start of each lineage and your Economy crests.

Bottom right: Population

On the left of each space, the Gold you pay to move up; on the right, the cards you draw at the start of each lineage and your Population crests.

Markers start on the empty space at the bottom of each track. Your crests are those of your current level: they do not add up from one level to the next.

6Lineages

A lineage begins when a player starts again with their full deck, and ends when they decide to end it, or when they win. Each player manages their lineages at their own pace: they drift apart from one player to the next during the game.

Starting a lineage

  1. Take the Gold shown by your Economy track, plus any Gold from your Privileges.

  2. Draw the number of cards shown by your Population track.

If this draws the last card of your deck, you win immediately, even if it is not your turn.

Your areas

Your draw pile: your deck, face down. Nobody knows its order.

Your hand: secret. The other players only know how many cards you hold.

Your play area: the cards you have played. They stay there until their resources are spent.

Your discard pile: the cards whose resources have been spent.

7Your turn

Starting with the first player, then clockwise, each player takes a single action from the following four. You may not pass.

Play 1 card

Place a card from your hand in your play area, then apply its effect if you wish.

Then each opponent, clockwise, may apply the card's Red Effect, even if you did not apply its effect.

Buy 1 card

Buy a card from the River by paying its cost. Gold comes from your supply. To pay Resources, discard cards from your play area that provide them.

The card you buy must replace a card of your deck, which is destroyed for free. It takes that card's place (see below). Then refill the River with the top card of the common deck.

Use a Privilege

If you have Privilege 13, 14 or 15, pay its cost to destroy or draw 1 card. This replaces your action: only once per turn, but you may do it again on later turns.

End your lineage

If you have 0 or 1 card in hand, you may end your lineage: your tracks move up, then you start again with your whole deck. See the next chapter.

Where the bought card goes

It takes the place of the card you destroy for it:

  • a card from your hand: the bought card goes into your hand, and you can play it on a later turn;
  • a card from your play area: the bought card goes into your play area. Its resources are available (those of the destroyed card are lost), but its effect is not triggered;
  • a card from your discard pile: the bought card goes into your discard pile and will only be used in your next lineage;
  • the top card of your draw pile, without looking at it: it is destroyed, and the bought card is placed in its stead, on top of your draw pile.

A purchase therefore never changes the number of cards in your deck. When the common deck is empty, the River is no longer refilled.

8Ending your lineage

With 0 or 1 card in hand, follow these steps in order. Each one is optional.

  1. Nobility. If your play area has enough Scholars for the first empty space of your Nobility track (1, then 2, 3 and 4), take one of the 4 face-up Privileges. Scholars are not spent. Reveal a new Privilege to replace it.

  2. Population. If you have enough Gold, pay the cost of the next level and move up one level.

  3. Economy. If your play area has enough Resources (Stone or Metal), move up one level. Resources are not spent.

  4. Leftover Gold. Discard all your remaining Gold (unless you have Privilege 18).

  5. New lineage. Shuffle your hand, play area and discard pile face down to form your deck again, then start a new lineage. On your next turn, you can play or buy a card.

Remember

Each track moves up only one level per lineage end, and you take only one Privilege.

9End of the game

The game ends immediately when a player:

  • draws the last card of their deck, in any way (effect, Search, Red Effect, start of a lineage);
  • or destroys the last card of their draw pile.

That player wins. There is only one winner: the other players are not ranked.

Ties

If several players draw their last card at the same time (through a Red Effect, for example), the player whose turn it is wins. If they are not among them, the first of them clockwise from the player who played the card wins.

10Reference

Card effects

  • All effects are optional. You may play a card without applying its effect.
  • When an effect has several parts (“Gain 1 Gold, then Buy 1 card”), they are independent: you may apply the second without the first.
  • When an effect counts something (resources, Scholars, crests), only count what the player applying it owns: their play area and their board. The card you have just played counts for its own effect.

Pay. Gold is taken from your supply. To pay Stone or Metal, discard cards from your play area that provide it. A discarded card is discarded entirely: its extra resources and Scholars are lost. The card you have just played may pay for its own effect.

Draw. Take the top card of your deck into your hand. When an effect makes you draw several cards, you draw them all at once.

Destroy. Permanently remove a card from your deck: from your hand, your play area, your discard pile, or the top card of your draw pile, without looking at it. You may destroy the card you have just played. If the effect destroys several cards, you may take the top of your draw pile several times, and you may destroy fewer than allowed.

Search. Look through your deck, choose 1 card and put it into your hand, then shuffle your deck. You may search for fewer cards than allowed.

Buy 1 card. A purchase on top of your action, exactly like the Buy action (normal cost, mandatory replacement). If there are several purchases, refill the River between them.

Level up. Move the stated track up one level without paying the board cost. A track never goes above level 4.

  • “Draw 1 card for every 2 Stone in your play area”: count the Stone present and apply the effect once per complete set. Nothing is spent.
  • “Pay 3 Metal to draw 1 card per Population crest”: pay only once, then draw as many cards as you have Population crests.
  • When an effect makes you pay and then counts, you count before paying: resources discarded to pay still count.
  • “Pay X Gold to draw (or destroy) 1 card”: only once per card played.
  • Market: sell as much Stone and Metal as you wish. Every resource on the discarded cards earns Gold: a card providing 1 Stone and 1 Metal earns twice.
Red Effects

When a player plays a card with a Red Effect, each of their opponents may apply it, one after the other, clockwise, starting with the next player. Each uses their own Gold, resources and board. The Red Effect can be applied even if the active player did not apply the card's effect.

Privileges

  • You get them when you end a lineage, thanks to Scholars, or through some cards (Religion, Chancellery, King's Court). You may own 4 at most.
  • A Privilege gained through a card also takes a space on your Nobility track: it requires no Scholars, but it gives that space's crests and makes the next Privilege harder to get.
  • Unless it says “at random from the pile”, you choose among the 4 face-up Privileges. When the Privilege pile is empty, they are no longer replaced.
  • Resources added by Privileges count everywhere: for paying, for tracks, for Privileges and for card effects.
